Data sources

Where our business listings come from, the licences we use them under, and how to fix or remove a listing.

1. Overture Maps Places data

Most listings on Harrow Online Hub started life as open data from the Overture Maps Foundation, a project that publishes free map data, including a worldwide dataset of places such as shops, restaurants and services.

Overture combines place data from several contributors. The Harrow listings we imported come from these sources:

  • BrightQuery
  • Meta
  • Microsoft
  • Uberall
  • DAC
  • PinMeTo

Each imported listing keeps its Overture ID and the date we imported it (visible to our team only), so we can update it from later Overture releases without creating duplicates.

3. How we chose which businesses to list

We started with every place Overture lists in Harrow and kept only the ones most likely to be real, open and useful to local people. A business is listed when it is:

  • inside the London Borough of Harrow, with a valid HA postcode and a phone number
  • not marked as permanently closed
  • a customer-facing business: we left out business-to-business services, corporate offices, car parks, parks, schools and government buildings
  • a confident match in Overture’s data (a confidence score of 0.8 or more, or 0.95 for office-type businesses)
  • found in more than just one less reliable source (records that came only from Foursquare or AllThePlaces were left out)
  • not named after a person, and not a chain (the same name at three or more locations, or the same website used by three or more names)
  • not a duplicate: where two records had the same name and postcode, we kept one

That left about 2,600 businesses. We then matched each one to one of our local categories and to an area (such as Pinner, Stanmore or Wealdstone) using its postcode and map pin.

4. What changes after import

Once a business claims its listing, the owner can correct and add details. We approve changes to business names. We may re-import Overture data from time to time to add new businesses and update unclaimed ones. Claimed listings are never overwritten by an import.

5. Imported listings and search engines

Imported listings are basic, and the details haven’t been confirmed by the business. So we ask search engines not to index them until the business has claimed the listing and filled it in. You can still find them by searching the Hub.

6. Correcting or removing a listing

If you spot a mistake, or you run a business and don’t want it listed, tell us using the correction and removal form. You’ll find a “Report a problem” link on every listing too. We aim to act within five working days.

If you own the business, the quickest way to fix it is to find your listing and claim it for free. You can then edit the details yourself.

For more on how we handle personal information in listings, see our privacy notice.
